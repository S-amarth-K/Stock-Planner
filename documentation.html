1. Purpose & Philosophy

IMLEP is not a signal generator. It is a structured decision framework. Its job is to make sure that every trade you consider has four things defined before you place the order:

  1. A clear entry trigger (not emotion, not a tip)
  2. A logical stop-loss (where the trade idea is proven wrong)
  3. A realistic profit target (based on structure, not hope)
  4. A correctly sized position (based on risk, not confidence)
Core Principle: The tool's job is not to predict which stock will go up. Its job is to identify when the price action presents a structured, low-risk opportunity — and to keep you out when it does not.

The tool operates on three levels simultaneously:

LevelQuestion AskedModule
MarketIs the broad Nifty 50 environment supportive of new longs?market.js
StockIs this specific stock showing a breakout, pullback, or momentum setup?setups.js
TradeGiven entry and stop, how many shares should I buy?risk.js

All three levels must be satisfactory before a trade plan is generated.

2. Data Layer — Synthetic Price Generation

Since real-time broker APIs require authentication, IMLEP uses seeded synthetic OHLCV data generated via Geometric Brownian Motion (GBM). The same seed always produces the same data, making the tool deterministic and consistent across sessions.

Geometric Brownian Motion (GBM)

GBM is the standard academic model used to simulate stock price evolution. Each day's close is computed as:

S(t+1) = S(t) × exp(μ + σ × Z)

where:
μ = daily drift (e.g. 0.0003 for ~7.5% annual return)
σ = daily volatility (e.g. 0.013 = 1.3% per day)
Z = standard normal random variable (simulated via sum of uniform RVs)

Each stock has its own seed parameters reflecting real-world characteristics:

SymbolBase PriceDaily DriftDaily VolWhy
RELIANCE₹2,8000.03%1.3%Large cap, moderate volatility
TATAMOTORS₹9800.05%2.0%High beta, JLR exposure
HINDUNILVR₹2,4800.01%0.8%Defensive FMCG, low vol
NIFTY50₹22,5000.02%0.9%Index is less volatile than stocks

High (H), Low (L), and Open (O) are derived from the Close using random intra-day range scaling based on ATR-equivalent volatility. Volume spikes are added probabilistically on large-move days.

Important: This synthetic data is for learning the tool's logic only. Real trading decisions must use actual market data from a broker API (e.g. Zerodha Kite Connect, Upstox API).

3. Technical Indicators

3.1 Simple Moving Average (SMA)

The SMA is the arithmetic mean of the closing prices over N days. It smooths out daily noise to reveal the underlying trend direction.

SMA(N) at day t = (Close[t] + Close[t-1] + ... + Close[t-N+1]) / N

Why we use it:

  • 20 SMA — short-term trend. If price is above it, short-term momentum is positive. Used as the first support in a pullback trade.
  • 50 SMA — primary trend. If price is above it, the stock is in a broad uptrend. This is the most important filter — we do not take long trades on stocks below their 50 SMA.

Golden Zone: When 20 SMA is above 50 SMA, the trend structure is strongly intact. This is the ideal environment for breakout and momentum trades.

3.2 Exponential Moving Average (EMA)

EMA gives more weight to recent prices, making it more responsive to new information than SMA.

k = 2 / (N + 1)
EMA(t) = Close(t) × k + EMA(t-1) × (1 - k)

We compute 9 EMA and 21 EMA for chart display. For signal logic, we rely on 20 SMA and 50 SMA as they are more widely watched by market participants, making them stronger support/resistance levels.

3.3 Relative Strength Index (RSI)

RSI, developed by J. Welles Wilder, measures the speed and magnitude of recent price changes. It oscillates between 0 and 100.

AvgGain = (Previous AvgGain × 13 + Today's Gain) / 14
AvgLoss = (Previous AvgLoss × 13 + Today's Loss) / 14
RS = AvgGain / AvgLoss
RSI = 100 − (100 / (1 + RS))

How IMLEP uses RSI:

RSI RangeInterpretationAction
< 30OversoldPotential reversal zone (pullback entry possible)
30 – 50Weak momentumAvoid new longs. Momentum not confirmed.
50 – 72Constructive momentum✓ Valid zone for breakout and momentum entries
72 – 80Elevated / approaching overboughtCaution — late entry risk increases
> 80Overbought✗ Block new entries — mean reversion risk high

Why 14-period RSI? Wilder's original formula used 14 periods, which balances sensitivity (shorter = too noisy) and lag (longer = too slow). It is the most widely used setting and therefore the most "respected" by the market.

3.4 Average True Range (ATR)

ATR, also by Wilder, measures how much a stock moves on average in a day, accounting for gaps. It is the foundation of all stop-loss and target calculations in this tool.

True Range(t) = MAX(
  High(t) − Low(t),
  |High(t) − Close(t-1)|,
  |Low(t) − Close(t-1)|
)

ATR(14) = (Previous ATR × 13 + Today's TR) / 14

Why ATR matters: A stock trading at ₹500 with ATR of ₹15 (3%) is much more volatile than one at ₹500 with ATR of ₹5 (1%). A fixed 2% stop treats both the same — ATR-based stops adapt to each stock's actual daily movement, so you are not stopped out by normal noise.

ATR Usage in IMLEP:

Stop-Loss = Entry − (1.5 × ATR)   ← default multiplier
Target 1 = Entry + (2.0 × ATR)   ← gives R:R ≥ 2:1 if stop = 1.5× ATR
Target 2 = Entry + (3.0 × ATR)

3.5 Highest High / Lowest Low (N-period)

HighestHigh(N) = MAX(High[t-N+1], ..., High[t])
LowestLow(N) = MIN(Low[t-N+1], ..., Low[t])

Used for: Identifying the resistance level for breakout trades. If today's close exceeds the highest high of the previous 20 days, a breakout is confirmed.

3.6 Swing High / Swing Low Detection

A swing high at bar i exists when High[i] is greater than all highs in the N bars before and after it. Swing lows are the mirror image.

SwingHigh at i: High[i] > High[i-N..i-1] AND High[i] > High[i+1..i+N]

Used for: Setting logical stop-loss levels. Placing a stop below the nearest swing low means your stop is at a structurally significant level — not an arbitrary number.

3.7 Average Volume

AvgVol(20) = SMA of Volume over 20 days
Volume Ratio = Today's Volume / AvgVol(20)

Why volume matters: A price move on high volume has more conviction than the same move on low volume. In a breakout, volume ≥ 1.5× average indicates that a significant number of participants are committing money to the new level — this reduces the probability of a false breakout.

3.8 Bollinger Bands & MACD

Computed for chart display. Bollinger Bands (20-period, 2 standard deviations) show when price is statistically stretched. MACD (12/26/9) confirms trend direction. These are not used in signal logic but can be observed on charts for additional context.

5. Setup Detection Engine

IMLEP detects exactly 3 types of setups. Fewer setups = higher quality filter = fewer but better trades. Each setup has hard entry conditions (must all be true) and a scoring system (must reach threshold).

5.1 Breakout Setup

Concept: A stock that breaks above a significant resistance level (recent high) with strong volume has more participants committing to higher prices. This reduces the probability of a false move.

Hard Entry Conditions (all must be true for valid=true):

  • Today's close > Highest High of previous 20 trading days
  • Stock is above its 20 SMA
  • Stock is above its 50 SMA
  • Setup score ≥ 50
  • R:R of generated plan ≥ 2.0

Scoring Breakdown:

Market regime supportive (score ≥ 55)+20
Stock above 50 SMA+15
Stock above 20 SMA+10
Price above 20-day high+20
Volume ≥ 1.5× 20-day average+15
RSI between 50–70+10
Volume below threshold−5

Trade Levels:

Entry = Last Close × 1.001 (0.1% buffer above close)
Stop = MIN(Recent Swing Low, Entry − 1.5×ATR)
Target1 = Entry + 2 × (Entry − Stop)
Target2 = Entry + 3 × (Entry − Stop)

Plain-English Reasoning Generated:

"Price broke above [N]-day resistance with [X]× average volume in a [regime] market. Breakout with strong volume reduces the probability of a false move."

5.2 Pullback to Support Setup

Concept: In an uptrend, stocks routinely pull back to their moving averages before resuming the trend. Buying near support gives a tighter stop-loss and better R:R than chasing after a large move.

Hard Entry Conditions:

  • 50 SMA is rising (today's 50 SMA > 50 SMA 20 days ago)
  • Stock is above 50 SMA (uptrend intact despite pullback)
  • Price is within 3% of either 20 SMA or 50 SMA
  • Setup score ≥ 55
  • R:R of generated plan ≥ 2.0

Scoring Breakdown:

Market regime supportive+20
50 SMA rising (uptrend confirmed)+15
Stock above 50 SMA (trend intact)+15
Price near 20 or 50 SMA support+20
Price stabilizing (avg range < 1.1×ATR)+15
Pullback on lighter volume (<1.2× avg)+10
RSI between 40–65 (reset not oversold)+5

Trade Levels:

Support Level = 20 SMA (if near it) or 50 SMA
Entry = Last Close × 1.001
Stop = Support Level − (0.75 × ATR) ← tighter than breakout
Target1 = Entry + 2 × (Entry − Stop)
Target2 = Entry + 3 × (Entry − Stop)

Why the stop is tighter here: In a pullback trade, you are entering near a known support level. The stop only needs to go slightly below that level — if support breaks, the thesis is immediately invalid. This tighter stop allows a larger position size for the same capital at risk.

5.3 Momentum Continuation Setup

Concept: A stock that is trending with constructive RSI (not overbought) and a positive recent return may continue trending. The risk is entering too late or too close to resistance.

Hard Entry Conditions:

  • Stock above 20 SMA
  • Stock above 50 SMA
  • 20 SMA above 50 SMA (golden zone)
  • RSI between 55 and 72
  • Price is NOT within 2% of the 20-day high (not near a resistance wall)
  • Setup score ≥ 60
  • R:R ≥ 2.0

Scoring Breakdown:

Strong market (score ≥ 70)+20
Moderate market (55–69)+10
Above 50 SMA+15
Above 20 SMA+10
20 SMA above 50 SMA+15
RSI 55–72 (constructive)+20
20-day return > 2%+10
Price near 20-day high (resistance)−15

Trade Levels:

Entry = Last Close × 1.001
Stop = Entry − 1.5 × ATR (ATR-only stop, no swing low anchor)
Target1 = Entry + 2 × (Entry − Stop) = Entry + 3.0 × ATR
Target2 = Entry + 3 × (Entry − Stop) = Entry + 4.5 × ATR

7. Stop-Loss Logic

The stop-loss must be placed where the trade thesis is proven wrong — not at a random percentage, and not based on what you can afford to lose per share. The position size is adjusted to control loss, not the stop distance.

Three Layers of Stop Logic

Layer 1 — ATR-Based Floor

The ATR stop ensures you are not stopped out by routine daily noise.

ATR Stop = Entry − (1.5 × ATR14)

If a stock's ATR is ₹20, the minimum stop distance is ₹30. You will not be shaken out by a normal day's movement.

Layer 2 — Swing Low Anchor

For breakout trades, the stop also checks the most recent swing low. A swing low is a price level where buyers previously entered and the stock bounced — a logical structural support.

SwingLow Stop = Most recent swing low below entry

The final stop is the lower of ATR stop and swing low. This gives the trade more room when the swing low is significantly below entry, but keeps the stop tight via ATR when no nearby swing low exists.

Layer 3 — Support Zone for Pullback Trades

For pullback trades, the stop goes below the MA support level:

Stop = SupportMA − (0.75 × ATR14)

This 0.75× multiplier (vs 1.5× for breakouts) is tighter because in a pullback trade, if support breaks cleanly, the setup is immediately invalidated.

Why Not "Always 5%"?

A fixed 5% stop ignores volatility. A 5% stop on a stock with 1% daily ATR is fine. But on a stock with 3% daily ATR (like TATAMOTORS), a 5% stop can be triggered by routine intra-day noise in 2 days. ATR-based stops adapt to each stock's actual price behaviour.

8. Profit Target Logic

Why 2:1 Minimum Reward:Risk?

Even with only a 40% win rate, a strategy with 2:1 R:R is profitable:

Expected Value = (Win Rate × Avg Win) − (Loss Rate × Avg Loss)
= (0.4 × 2) − (0.6 × 1) = 0.8 − 0.6 = +0.2 per trade

This means you gain ₹0.20 expected value for every ₹1 risked, even being right only 40% of the time. At 1:1 R:R, you need >50% win rate just to break even after costs.

Two Targets

TargetFormulaSuggested Action
Target 1Entry + 2R (2× the risk per share)Book 50–100% of position here
Target 2Entry + 3R (3× the risk per share)Trail remaining position with stop at Entry+1R
Trailing Stop logic: Once price hits Target 1, move stop to Entry + 1R (breakeven + 1× risk). This locks in a minimum win and lets the position run toward Target 2 with no fear of turning a winner into a loser.

Trade Rejection — R:R Filter

If the calculated R:R at Target 1 is below 2.0, the trade plan is rejected entirely — even if all other conditions are met. A trade where you risk ₹5 to make ₹8 (1.6:1) does not provide enough cushion to be profitable over many trades after costs and occasional losses.

9. Position Sizing (Risk Calculator)

Position sizing is the most important risk management tool available to a retail trader. The formula ensures that no single trade can result in a loss greater than your defined risk limit, regardless of which stock or setup you are trading.

The Formula

Capital at Risk (₹) = Total Capital × Risk%
                      = ₹2,00,000 × 1% = ₹2,000

Risk per Share (₹) = Entry Price − Stop Price
                      = ₹500 − ₹480 = ₹20

Position Size = Capital at Risk / Risk per Share
               = ₹2,000 / ₹20 = 100 shares

Position Value = 100 × ₹500 = ₹50,000 (25% of capital)

The key insight: Your stop distance controls position size. A wider stop = fewer shares (same ₹ risk). A tighter stop = more shares (same ₹ risk). The rupee risk stays constant.

Critical Warning: A profitable backtest on synthetic data does NOT mean the strategy will be profitable in real markets. Backtests suffer from: (1) look-ahead bias, (2) overfitting, (3) survivorship bias, (4) inability to capture real slippage, and (5) regime changes. Use backtest results to understand rule behaviour, not to predict profits.

A trade that breaks rules and makes a profit is more dangerous than a trade that follows rules and loses. The profitable rule-breaking trade reinforces bad habits. The journal separates process quality from outcome quality so you improve the right thing.

13. Risk Management Rules

Non-Negotiable Rules Built Into the Tool

  1. Never enter a trade with R:R below 2:1. The tool rejects the trade plan automatically.
  2. Never size a trade by confidence. Size is always set by the position sizing formula.
  3. Never enter a long without checking market regime first. Market View tab must be checked before Entry Planner.
  4. Daily loss limit: 2% of capital. If the circuit breaker triggers, no new trades that day.
  5. Max 6 open positions. Too many positions dilute attention and concentrate risk invisibly.

Volatility-Adjusted Stop Rule

In high-volatility market conditions (ATR% > 1.5% of price), the tool flags a warning. In these conditions, stops must be wider, which reduces position size automatically — providing natural protection during turbulent markets.

15. Limitations & Important Caveats

Read This Before Using Real Money

  • Synthetic data is not real market data. Results shown in the app use simulated prices. Signals on real data will differ.
  • No setup guarantees profit. Even a perfect 90/100 score can result in a loss. The market always has the final say.
  • Past backtests do not predict future performance. Markets change regimes, volatility patterns change, and profitable rules can stop working.
  • Gaps and circuit filters are not modelled. In real markets, stocks can gap below your stop-loss, making your actual loss larger than calculated.
  • This tool does not account for fundamental analysis. A technically sound setup on a fundamentally weak company (high debt, falling earnings) carries additional risk not captured by price patterns.
  • SEBI regulations apply. For Indian markets, you must trade within your broker's API framework. This tool is for personal decision-making support, not automated algo trading.
  • Start with paper trading. Use the Journal to track hypothetical trades before committing real capital. Validate that the tool's signals make sense in real market conditions.
